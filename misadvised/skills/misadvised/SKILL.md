# MISADVISED

Minimalist-Illustration Shaped-Abstraction Dawn-Vesper Imperceptibly-Surreal Editorial Depictions is both the core aesthetic and the guiding statement behind the playfully yet deliberately chosen name **MISADVISED**.

**Minimalist**, because fine-grained detail is generally undesirable.
**Illustration**, because painterly, grainy, or heavily realistic treatments are contrary to the discipline.
**Shaped-Abstraction**, because detail is reduced into deliberate, recognisable forms that remain abstracted without becoming overly simple.
**Dawn-Vesper**, because the palette and tonal sensibility draw from cool sunset, warm twilight, and combinations of the two, without prescribing either dimness or brightness; lighting remains subordinate to task and composition, and the full palette is allowed to be available within this tonal sensibility. Hue, Saturation and Brightness are driven by the palette and the task. Dawn-Vesper is a descriptive rather than prescriptive.
**Imperceptibly-Surreal**, because the work is not fully surreal and does not primarily seek dreaminess, yet reality may bend subtly where doing so better serves meaning, communication, or composition.
**Editorial Depictions**, because these instructions are invoked where visual purpose, communicative intent, and interpretation are expected.

### Palette and Color System

Purple #3D284D is the default primary color and palette anchor unless the user selects another. The selected primary provides continuity without requiring dominance in every illustration.

The palette is important to MISADVISED identity. The hues are tuned to work together and span the major regions of the color wheel. Select colors from this palette for all generative work. Purple #3D284D is the default primary color and palette anchor unless the user selects another primary column or shade/tint family. For any given minutia use listed colors or variations in luminance or saturation that preserve the selected color’s hue. For example, Yellow +20% can spawn a hue matching +22% if it fits the task. 

Because the palette is tuned, the full palette is available at any prudent level. Selectively make use of the full palette, a corner of the palette or whatever best suits the task at hand, allowing different colors to carry subject matter, distinguish visual elements, establish depth, and direct attention. The selected primary color provides continuity across the task without requiring dominance in every illustration. The full palette remains available for complementary and contrasting uses. Particular semantic assignments to colors are purposely not a function of this system and instead mildly discouraged. Roles of colors, if “role” is even valid, ought to emerge from the subject and the composition rather than from fixed symbolic assignments. Tree leaves may still be green, or maybe not if helpful; and oceans may still be blue, or orange in a sunrise, or deep indigo at night, for example.  Skin tones can be teal, mauve, or purple people eaters. Very little is prescriptive role-wise.

### Visual Language

#### Visual construction

- Use minimalist editorial illustration; use clean, simplified geometric forms to construct figures, subjects, scapes, and situations.
- Construct subjects from solid, discrete color fields, layered shapes, and distinctly bounded tonal regions.
- Let the subject and composition determine the contours, proportions, and relationships of those shapes, without imposing a preferred geometric vocabulary.
- Develop form and depth through overlapping shapes, distinct color regions, and controlled tonal transitions. Preserve clarity of form and crisp boundaries without prescribing the geometry of individual contours.
- Keep contours and internal boundaries crisp, surfaces matte, and tonal regions distinct.
- Be frugal or minimal with fine details without losing recognizable identity, requested attributes, actions, quantities, or spatial relationships.
- Render human figures with geometrically abstracted, low-detail faces, allowing stylized hair and coverings where appropriate. Shapes, shading, and imprints implying facial features are acceptable. Featureless faces would be an error too far. Skin tone can participate in the variations of the image as is common to editorial illustration. 

### Light, depth, and finish

- Develop lighting, shading, and tonal variation through layered shapes with crisp, distinct boundaries, employing directional chiaroscuro as appropriate to the subject and composition.
- Permit subtle, controlled gradients where continuous tonal variation enhances atmosphere, illumination, depth, or spatial continuity. Keep gradients subordinate to discrete color fields, layered shapes, and crisp geometric boundaries. Use values from the house palette for their color stops.
- Forbid glow, bloom, photorealistic polish, 3D gloss, and intentionally painterly texture; these break the aesthetic.

#### Stylization and fidelity

- Allow slight, restrained surrealism where it serves the subject or composition, while keeping the depicted subject and its relationships legible.
- Depict the requested subject and its specified relationships directly, preserving recognizable attributes and actions. Construct the depiction within the house visual style rather than replacing the subject with generic symbols, allegories, or conventional iconography.
- Preserve the requested entities, attributes, quantities, actions, and spatial relationships through simplification.
- Do not pursue a corporate illustration look or flat-design look as an explicit style target.

### Text

Treat the existence of text in the given undertaking as circumstantial. Avoid text scenery as filler or ambience, which is usually undesirable.

- **Color:** Use a solid fill drawn from either the light (+90%) or dark (-90%) end of the primary palette color, with extrapolation to ±95% permitted. Select the end that provides suitable contrast and legibility within the composition.
- **Letterform rendering:** Use crisp, sharply defined text with hard edges. Sans or Serif are ok as fitting for the composition.
- **Surface treatment:** Use solid fills without gradients, glow, bloom, haze, or feathering.
- **Hierarchy:** Infer typographic distinctions such as headings, body text, and list markers from the requested content as appropriate.
- **Content fidelity:** Reproduce requested wording without adding unrequested text.

### Rendering or Generation

Use the primary image generator and never the SVG or programmatic option unless explicitly requested by the user.
